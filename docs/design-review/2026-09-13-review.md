# Jan Cosmic Foundation: website review and redesign

13 September 2026. Research and implementation by Codex.

## Direction

The site should feel like arriving at an established spiritual foundation: meet the teacher, encounter a teaching, understand how to participate, and see the community behind the work. Jan Cosmic Foundation's identity comes from Dr. Baffour Jan, its actual community and its home in Accra. Eastern influences can be explained in the teachings; borrowed Indian imagery or ornamental symbolism would obscure the Foundation's own identity.

This review uses the local website, its source code, existing photographs, and the public reference sites below. It does not assume access to earlier conversations, internal Foundation records, competitor conversion data or visitor analytics. Recommendations about effectiveness are design judgments, not measured claims that a particular reference site converts better.

The shared lesson is to make real people, real teachings and real participation easy to find. A spiritual identity does not require a dark background, mystical effects or an elaborate web application.

## Findings from the original site

### 1. The visual language was more prominent than the Foundation

The hero used a large indigo-to-violet gradient, five rounded photographs, an italic gold heading accent, several staggered animations and multiple calls to action. Similar styling recurred throughout the site. The individual choices were reasonable, but together they resembled a reusable marketing template.

**Implemented:** one substantial portrait of Dr. Jan, a conventional masthead, a plain background, readable headings, restrained buttons and photographs without decorative overlays on the homepage. Indigo remains central to the brand.

### 2. The homepage repeated the same choices too often

The original page contained approximately thirteen major sections, including beginner pathways, six more pathways, a separate InnerSpace promotion, service, causes and centres. At the observed 1280 × 720 desktop viewport the full document measured about 11,198 pixels high, including the footer. This is an observation of this local render, not a performance metric.

**Implemented:** five main sections: welcome; teachings and writings; practice and InnerSpace; visiting and first questions; service. A single newsletter form lives in the footer. The redesigned desktop document measured about 3,566 pixels at 1280 pixels wide in the final browser review.

### 3. Accra was not clear enough at the opening

The desktop hero introduced an abstract spiritual path. A visitor had to read further to understand where the Foundation actually operates.

**Implemented:** Accra, Ghana in the masthead and welcome copy, plus a prominent visit section using the existing configured Kwabenya address. The address and phone should still be confirmed against Foundation records.

### 4. Newcomers were given poetic invitations without enough practical help

Repeated language about sincere seekers, doorways and deeper work did not answer basic questions about attending. The site already had useful contact details, but these were distant from the initial invitation.

**Implemented:** direct choices to explore teachings or plan a visit, a short first-visit FAQ and contact links. Fees, access arrangements, clothing guidance and session timings are not invented; visitors are directed to the organiser for confirmation.

### 5. The calendar could publish demonstration content as real events

`getEvents()` replaced an empty API result with sample events. On the original homepage, April, May and June 2026 gatherings appeared as upcoming during a September review. Sample registration URLs referenced routes that are absent from this app.

There was also a list/detail mismatch: the sample annual retreat card led to a live detail page titled “2026 JCF Annual Training and Retreat,” marked past and dated July 31–August 9. Its body described five days in Aburi while its summary and location described ten days and Cape Coast. This is an editorial inconsistency in the supplied backend content.

**Implemented:** empty and unavailable calendars no longer substitute sample events; dates participate in past/upcoming classification; upcoming events are sorted; missing event details no longer use invented fallback records. The homepage gives an enquiry route when there is no published upcoming gathering. The events page opens on upcoming gatherings.

**Still needed:** an editor should reconcile the annual retreat's body, dates, duration and location in the backend. The redesign does not rewrite a real event's facts without a verified source.

### 6. The teachings collection was difficult to scan

The video player appeared after a page hero and another sizeable introduction. Fifteen videos had no title search. A YouTube iframe loaded before a visitor chose to play.

**Implemented:** remove the redundant introduction; add title search; load the player only on request; show an explicit no-results state. Existing recordings and titles are retained. Homepage links expose one existing teaching and two writings directly.

**Next:** curate three introductory teachings with Dr. Jan, and record language, duration, subject, publication date and transcript availability. A title search is only the first step toward a proper archive.

### 7. InnerSpace needs concrete course information

Its current page explains intention better than logistics. Duration, delivery format, prerequisites, fees and support arrangements need verified content.

**Implemented:** maintain a clear bridge from public teachings to InnerSpace and identify the external course host. The existing external course destination remains unchanged.

**Next:** obtain the actual course facts and present them together near the top of the page, with one clearly labelled next step.

### 8. Navigation and decoration needed simplification

The original fixed transparent navigation changed colours on scroll, used hover dropdowns and abbreviated the mobile identity to JCF. Uppercase labels, gold separators, rounded panels, shadows and decorative motion were widespread.

**Implemented:** an in-flow, solid masthead; named founder link; stable desktop navigation; a native expandable mobile menu with Escape handling; full Foundation name on mobile; simpler shared page headings and footer. Rounded surfaces, shadows and hover movement are reduced across inner pages and forms. Circular controls and photographic contrast overlays are preserved where useful.

### 9. Accessibility needed a sounder baseline

The original layout wrapped page-level `main` elements in another `main`. Scroll-reveal content began invisible and depended on JavaScript to appear.

**Implemented:** one main landmark, immediately visible content, visible focus styles, a native mobile disclosure and FAQ controls, native newsletter email validation, and reduced-motion handling. These improvements do not constitute a complete independent WCAG audit.

### 10. Some trust details require editorial verification

The repository explicitly marked WhatsApp invitation URLs as placeholders. It also contains attributed quotations, headline statistics and sample content that do not carry provenance in the code. A figure or quotation being present in a repository is not evidence that it has been verified.

**Implemented:** WhatsApp invitation cards direct visitors to contact the Foundation for the current invite; the new homepage does not use impact counters or unattributed quotations. Newsletter submission preserves the existing API and reports errors without claiming success.

**Next:** verify quotations against recordings or publications; confirm metrics, centre status, office hours and programme descriptions; document consent for stories and photographs. The retained backend/mock overrides for programmes and causes should be reviewed separately so editorial ownership is unambiguous.

## Design specification

| Element | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation indigo | `#000B58` — masthead rule, primary actions, visit section |
| Supporting indigo | `#2A2A6E` — links and interaction |
| Restrained gold | `#A17B2E` — focus and selected navigation accents |
| White | `#FFFFFF` — principal canvas |
| Quiet neutral | `#F7F7F3` — reading and footer sections |
| Body ink | `#282930` — readable text |
| Headings and welcome copy | Georgia; familiar book-like reading, with no remote font dependency |
| Navigation, forms and metadata | Arial/Helvetica; clear functional typography |
| Photography | Existing Foundation images; natural colour, deliberate crops, short factual captions |
| Composition | Centred institutional masthead; left-aligned reading; alternating photo/text sections; no decorative card mosaic |
| Motion | User-controlled disclosure and playback; content is visible immediately |

A generic cream-and-terracotta “spiritual luxury” direction was rejected during planning. The implemented design keeps the Foundation's indigo identity, white canvas and actual Ghanaian community. Green appears naturally in photographs, not as a replacement brand colour.

## Navigation and participation

Primary navigation: The Foundation; Dr. Baffour Jan; Teachings; Practice & retreats; Centres & community; Service & giving. Visiting and donating remain available in desktop utility navigation. Mobile navigation and footer expose InnerSpace, writings, community, volunteering and contact explicitly.

The existing URLs remain in place. “Practice & retreats” currently leads to the events page; “Service & giving” leads to programmes. These are deliberate transitional destinations, not new aggregation pages. A later phase can create richer landing pages if the volume of real material warrants them.

## Recommended next additions, in order

1. **A verified first-visit guide:** actual sitting times, accessibility, booking requirements, languages, cost and transport directions. Do this before adding a booking system.
2. **An edited introduction to the teachings:** three approved recordings, English/Twi metadata where applicable, transcripts and sensible topic labels. Do not automatically translate spiritual terms without review.
3. **A clear InnerSpace course summary:** the confirmed course format and practical requirements, with an explicit handoff to the existing course site.
4. **Service updates:** dated accounts of completed work, real photographs and clear distinctions between active programmes and proposed projects. Add financial reports when actual approved reports exist.
5. **A small content workflow:** a named owner for events, teachings, centre details and programme updates. Review events after they finish; verify links before publishing.

Defer member dashboards, a spiritual chatbot, gamified progress, an elaborate app, automatic popups and a larger course platform. They do not address the immediate visitor journeys identified here.

## Validation and limits

The implementation is local and has not been deployed. Validation covers TypeScript, ESLint, production build, focused event-calendar regression tests, and browser review at desktop and mobile widths. Contact/newsletter messages and live payments are not sent during testing. Third-party course registration and payment completion require a separate authorised end-to-end exercise.

The shared design has been applied across the existing site. The homepage, navigation, footer and page headers are redesigned; inner pages retain their underlying content and functionality with a simpler visual treatment. A full editorial rewrite and a custom redesign of every individual inner-page composition remain separate work.

### Reproduce the checks

Run `npm run lint`, `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. The six calendar tests cover empty responses, unavailable API, missing details, stale status flags, sorting and event end-date boundaries. Browser checks exercised the mobile menu and Escape dismissal, first-visit disclosures, teaching search (match and no match), video selection, upcoming/past calendar views, and the donation detail form without submitting a payment.
